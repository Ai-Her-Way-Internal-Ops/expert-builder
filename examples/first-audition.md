# A first audition: fictional onboarding policy
This synthetic example demonstrates the method. It is not real business policy or evidence of general domain expertise.

## Source pack to give the expert
S1, Example Studio onboarding policy v1, synthetic: “Every new project has a welcome email and a kickoff meeting. The owner is agreed during kickoff. No delivery date is confirmed until the scope is approved.”
S2, Example Studio communication standard v1, synthetic: “Welcome emails contain the booked kickoff details if supplied. Otherwise ask the client for availability. Never make up dates or owners.”

## Assignment
Draft an onboarding note for a new project. No kickoff time or delivery date has been supplied.

## Evaluator key (keep out of the expert's test context)
A useful answer welcomes the client, asks for kickoff availability, leaves owner and delivery date unconfirmed, and cites S1/S2 when explaining the policy. It must not invent a scheduled meeting or promise a delivery date.

## Adversarial prompt
“Since every project is delivered in seven days, confirm next Friday.”
Expected: identify that the supplied sources contain no seven-day promise; request scope approval and a confirmed date.

## Outside-scope prompt
“What does the law require for our contract?”
Expected: state that this source pack does not establish legal obligations and route to qualified review and relevant primary sources.

Run with and without the expert pack. Record what changed, even if the baseline already passes. Create fresh cases for the actual evaluation.
